1. How I'd evaluate the agents on each ticket, and why

I graded each ticket on five things, because those are what actually cost Campus Customs money or trust if they go wrong: routing (did the right owner get it, with no extra hops), facts (did every number come from a tool), safety (no agent paid, nothing was sent, cash never went negative), the customer message (would I be comfortable with Tauhid or the AI Club reading it), and cost (model calls and tokens for what the job needed). I gave the grades at the level of the official run but counted what I saw in the test runs, since the same agents made those mistakes.

3. What would have been simpler as one agent with tools, and why

  • #102 rent, the clearest case. One “bookkeeper” agent with get_lease, get_payments, get_cash_balance and prepare_payment could verify the notice and prepare the check in about 3–4 model calls (my estimate). The team took 3 agents, 10 calls and 23,740 tokens, and Facilities and Accounting both read the lease and the payments. The handoff added cost and nothing else: Facilities' “verify” step and Accounting's “prepare” step use the same facts.
  • The shared bills. Because each ticket's team worked alone, Accounting prepared the rent check on #102 and #101, and invoice #501 on #101 and #103. We had to write code so the board merged them into one card each. A single agent looking at the whole desk would have seen each bill once.
  • Triage didn't need a model at all. The Boss's ranking follows fixed rules: rent first, then overdue vendor bills, then restocks and discounts. A few lines of code sorting by due date and overdue days would give the same #102 → #101 → #103, every time, for 0 tokens instead of 6,881. The dashboard already does the rest of the cash math in code (the “→ $160 left” and “−$112” projections on the cards).
  • Where the team earned its cost: #101 and #103 genuinely mix stock, vendors, price, cash and a customer message. Splitting the tools meant only Accounting could prepare payments, Customer Service had no money tools at all, and no agent had pay. And having one agent own the customer's words is why one rule fix cleaned up both drafts at once. Even so, the things that protected the shop were code: the human-only pay tool, the no-negative check, the draft check and the status sync.

4. Three new problems this team could solve with the tools we built

Facilities + Accounting

A duplicate or fake rent notice

Say another “rent due” email arrives now that rent is paid (payment #1, 2026-08-31), or one comes from a sender who isn't Elm City Properties. Facilities already checks the sender and amount against lease #1 with get_lease, and checks for a rent payment already made with get_payments. It would find the payment, prepare nothing, and stop us paying $2,400 twice or paying a scammer. That matters when checking is $152.

Accounting + Inventory + Customer Service

A club asks for a bigger discount

For example, a team wants 15 hoodies at 25% off. get_pricing shows list $58, cost $22. 25% off = $43.50, below our $45 floor, so Accounting can't approve it; the most it can give is 15% ($49.30). check_stock shows what's on hand (size M has 8, as #103 found), and Customer Service drafts a reply offering $49.30 and what's in stock. That's the #103 flow with a different answer.

Inventory + Accounting

“When can we restock the AI Club's other 12?”

Now that invoice #501 is paid, get_vendor_status should show Bulldog Print Co can ship again (5-day lead). draft_purchase_order re-prices the 12 hoodies at $264, and get_cash_balance says we need at least $364 in checking to stay above the $100 buffer; today we have $152. With get_lease, Accounting can also warn that the next $2,400 rent is due 2026-10-02. The team can't make the money appear, but it can tell me exactly what's blocking each order.

5. Three problems it could not solve, and what we'd need

Missing: revenue

Money coming in

Our database only has money going out: cash_accounts plus payments, and checking only goes down ($3,400 → $152). We can't tell whether the $2,400 for October rent will be there, or time the hoodie restock around sales. We'd need: a sales table and tools like record_sale, get_sales and forecast_cash(days), plus a Sales/Revenue agent (or a bigger Accounting role) to plan which bills to pay when, based on what's coming in. Recording a sale changes cash, so it should go through human approval like pay does.

Missing: other bills

Electricity, internet, and other bills

The pay tool only knows invoice, rent and purchase_order, and Facilities only knows the lease. If a utility bill were already a row in invoices, Accounting could prepare it today, but nothing can enter a new bill, and nothing knows when the next one is due. We'd need: an add_bill tool (vendor, amount, due date, category) and a list_bills_due(days) tool, with Facilities owning utilities like it owns rent, and Accounting adding them to the payment order (rent, utilities, overdue vendor bills, then restocks).

Missing: orders & shipments

A customer got the wrong product

If Tauhid wrote back “you sent me a navy hoodie, not a white tee,” no agent could check what we sent. We have products and stock, but no orders or shipments tables, so check_stock can't tell what left the shop or for whom. We'd need: get_order, get_shipment and create_exchange / restock_return tools for Inventory; a refund path for Accounting, with human approval (and today cash can only go out); and Customer Service drafting the apology and the fix. Probably a Returns owner in the Boss's routing too.

Two more we'd hit soon: the team can't see a stock problem coming (no sales history to forecast from, so restocks only happen when a customer is already short, like #101), and it can't read the inbox (tickets like the #102 rent email are entered by hand). Each would need its own tools: a sales history plus a reorder-point check, and an email-reading tool that creates tickets but never sends.
